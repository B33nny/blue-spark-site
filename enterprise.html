Your business is not standard. Your automation should not be either.

Discuss a Blue Spark engagement built around your workflows, systems, people, and deployment requirements.

  1. Understand the work

  2. Agree scope

  3. Implement

  4. Verify against the agreed outcome

This describes how an engagement is discussed and delivered; it is not a required procurement sequence.

Start with the work—not a licence count.

Which process needs improving? Where does information move? What decisions belong to the team? We begin with the business outcome and define the work required to support it.

Configure for your organization.

Individual context, shared company knowledge, departmental access, integrations, and deployment arrangements depend on the engagement. They should match your requirements rather than force every organization into the same model.

Implementation is real work.

Discovery, workflow design, configuration, integration, and hands-on support are scoped and priced as part of the engagement. On-site work can be discussed where appropriate.

A pilot when it helps. A full deployment when it fits.

A paid pilot can establish a smaller starting scope. An organization ready for a full engagement does not need a compulsory pilot merely to enter the sales process.

Let your people deal with people. Let Blue Spark deal with the computer.
