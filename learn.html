You know the work. Start there.

You do not need to learn everything about AI before asking it to help.

Choose a task worth handing over.

Start with something that returns to your day: client preparation, research, a document, or a reporting routine. Pick an example whose result you know how to judge.

You are not trying to redesign the whole business. You are finding one useful place to begin.

Give the result a shape.

Explain what you need, what information matters, and how the finished work should look.

"Use these notes to prepare a one-page meeting brief. Include the background, open questions, and decisions for tomorrow. Leave out anything not supported by the notes."

That is already a useful starting point.

Make it yours.

Read the first result. Explain what to keep and what to change: the length, the tone, the detail, the order.

"I need the decisions first. Keep the background shorter. Separate confirmed dates from suggested dates."

You are explaining your standards, not learning a programming language.

Find the part that repeats.

Which inputs change next time? Which instructions remain useful? Where should your judgment stay involved?

Those answers help you develop a repeatable workflow around supported tools and tasks.

Keep practice comfortable.

Use representative material you are allowed to process. Keep confidential work out of public demonstrations. Check the important facts before using an output with a client.
