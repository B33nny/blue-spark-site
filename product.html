01 — A working day, four rooms

Each room names the work and the moment.

You read the room that matches your day. Skip the others.

THE IP ROOM — WEDNESDAY AFTERNOON

The deposition binder is open. Three exhibits, a witness statement, a draft response to opposing counsel.

Pull every place Exhibit B and the witness statement contradict each other.

Blue Spark reads the binder against the statement, surfaces the contradictions in order, and cites the page numbers. You keep drafting. The binder never left the disk on your desk.

What's left for you is the response itself.

THE FINANCE ROOM — END OF MONTH

Nine tabs open. Three spreadsheets. A reconciliation that's off by $412.

Reconcile these against the master, line by line. Tell me where the gap is and which line items to question first.

Blue Spark works the spreadsheets against the master, names the gap, ranks the line items by likelihood. You confirm and write the email to the controller.

What's left for you is the email.

A COPYWRITER'S DESK — FRIDAY BEFORE LAUNCH

A long brief. Voice notes from three calls. Three half-finished drafts.

Read my drafts. Tighten the second one into the voice of the first, but don't lose the specificity of the third.

Blue Spark reads all three, proposes a tightened version, keeps the rhythm. You accept the lines that work, rewrite the ones that don't.

What's left for you is the voice.

A SOVEREIGN BUILDER'S MACHINE — SUNDAY MORNING

A local repo. A draft ADR. A half-finished runbook for the install.

Read the ADR. Generate a checklist the operator can actually run.

Blue Spark reads the document, drafts the checklist, and asks you to confirm the steps that should require a kill switch.

What's left for you is the decisions.

02 — What the product does, in plain English

No marketing language. One verb per line.

Voice

Local speech-to-text, local text-to-speech. Low latency, scaled to your hardware.

Memory

Five layers: working, episodic, semantic, procedural, persona. The product remembers you across sessions, fiscal periods, case files.

Brain

Routes a question to the local model or to the cloud. You don't choose. The product decides.

Hands

A worker process for the actions you consent to: keyboard, browser, files. Asks before anything outside the grant.

Bridge

The registry of tools and external API hooks you trust the product to touch.

Controls

Permissions and the audit ledger that records them. The kill switch that stops outbound action. The hardware toggle that cuts the mic at the device.

Skills

Persona and persona revisions. Yours. You can change it whenever you want.

Status

System health, memory state, license, version. One panel. Visible any time.

Update

Auto-update. Signed installer. Quiet by default.

One persistent brain loop. Nine capabilities. That's the line.

03 — What it isn't

Four axes. No trash talk.

On practical office automation

Cloud AI is capable but disconnected from what's on your disk. Apple Intelligence is mostly OS-level suggestion. Ollama is flexible but DIY. Blue Spark is built for the daily backlog — the spreadsheet, the binder, the brief.

On memory

Cloud AI forgets you per session. Apple Intelligence has limited context and no persistent memory. Ollama has no persistent memory at all. Blue Spark remembers you across sessions, months, fiscal periods.

On sovereignty

Cloud AI is a round-trip; you are training the next model unless you opt out. Apple Intelligence is on-device but locked to Apple hardware. Ollama is sovereign but DIY. Blue Spark keeps voice and data on your machine.

On the hardware barrier

Cloud AI runs in someone else's data center. Apple Intelligence needs Apple silicon. Ollama needs a GPU-class machine. Blue Spark runs on the office laptop you already own.

04 — The same product, on a Saturday

The same sovereignty that protects a case file protects the will you draft on Saturday morning. The same memory that remembers a fiscal period remembers a school calendar. The same hardware toggle that cuts the microphone on your laptop cuts it on the tablet.

The product doesn't know the difference between professional and personal. That's the point.

Your machine is your machine, across the week.
